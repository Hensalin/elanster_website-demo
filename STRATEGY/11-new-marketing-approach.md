# 11 · New marketing approach: "Proof by process"

## The problem
After studying 20 competitors, nearly all win trust with named logos, certifications and metrics. Elanster has none of those to show yet, and inventing them is off the table. Competing on their ground loses.

## The idea
**Make the way you work the proof.** Buyers fear paying for a prototype that never ships, vague estimates, status-meeting theatre and a vendor that disappears after launch. Elanster can answer each fear with something checkable *before* a contract:

| Buyer fear | Checkable answer (all from existing Elanster facts) |
|---|---|
| "Will this ship?" | Deployed on real infrastructure in 4 to 12 weeks, not a prototype |
| "Will estimates hold?" | Honest estimate on the call; what won't fit is said out loud |
| "Will I see progress?" | Working software every Friday; Slack and repos shared |
| "Will you vanish?" | Accountable after launch; people who build stay on it |
| "Is the call a sales pitch?" | 30 min with a lead architect; follow-up notes in 24 hours; leaves with a first milestone |
| "Is AI even right for me?" | Say so honestly; readiness review first if unsure |

Second lever: **candour**. Vstorm and Bacancy show it works. "If AI isn't the right answer yet, we'll tell you" is cheap, differentiating and consistent with the Integrity value and the readiness-review service.

Third lever: **start with the pain, not the technology.** The Work cards already open with problems ("Too many documents sorted by hand?"). Extend that to headings.

## Offer ladder (lead capture without fake popups)
1. **Project-fit tool** (built): zero commitment, tells the visitor a next step.
2. **AI readiness review** (service exists; needs a dedicated page, price/duration set by you).
3. **Strategy call** (30 min, built into every CTA).
4. *(Needs your decision)* Free "map one workflow" session, as Vstorm does.

## Content engine (needs real authors)
Pillars: *Production vs prototype*, *Where to keep the human*, *RAG after the demo*, *Two meetings a week*. One strong article per month beats six samples. Distribute through LinkedIn (your only stated channel) and a Clutch/GoodFirms profile.

## Do not do
Invent logos, ratings or numbers; claim "#1"; use speed gimmicks ("15 days"); state IP ownership or pricing terms you haven't decided.

## Decisions needed from you
1. Approve the candour claims ("we'll tell you if AI isn't right", "sometimes strong engineering, not AI") as company policy.
2. Decide code/IP ownership wording at handover.
3. Decide whether to offer a free workflow-mapping session or paid readiness review.
4. Provide any one referenceable client or quote, even anonymised, with permission.
