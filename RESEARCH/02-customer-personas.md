## Persona A — The AI-curious operator (primary)
- Role: COO / head of operations / product owner at an SMB or mid-market firm.
- Problem: manual document, support or back-office work; unsure where AI truly pays off.
- Motivation: save staff time without a risky science project.
- Fears: prototype that never ships; hype; data security; vendor lock-in.
- Needs before contacting: what the engagement looks like, timeline, who does the work, whether people stay in the loop.
- Convincing signal: a concrete first milestone and an honest estimate.
- Where Elanster speaks to them: two-meeting cadence, humans in the loop, readiness review.

## Persona B — The founder with an AI idea
- Role: startup founder, often non-technical.
- Problem: needs a deployed MVP fast, with limited budget.
- Fears: wrong architecture, overpaying, being locked to an agency.
- Needs: timeline, process visibility, who owns the code.
- Signal: "4 to 12 weeks", weekly demos, a defined first release.

## Persona C — The engineering lead who needs capacity
- Role: CTO / VP Engineering with a small team.
- Problem: team lacks LLM/RAG/agent experience or capacity.
- Fears: quality drop, process friction, code they can't maintain.
- Needs: code-review standards, how the team embeds in Slack and repos, tech stack specifics.
- Signal: technical depth on the blog and in case studies. **This is currently the weakest area:** the site names no frameworks or cloud providers.

## Journey (all personas)
1. Search or referral ("AI development company", "RAG pipeline development", "AI MVP development") →
2. Home: understands the offer in about five seconds →
3. Looks for proof: work, clients, process →
4. Low-commitment step: project-fit tool, article, FAQ →
5. High-commitment step: book a 30-minute strategy call.

Biggest drop-off risks: no proof at step 3; no alternative to a call at step 5; no contact email.

## Information the business should supply to sharpen these
Past client types and sizes; won/lost reasons; typical project sizes; which segment is most profitable; any referenceable clients.
